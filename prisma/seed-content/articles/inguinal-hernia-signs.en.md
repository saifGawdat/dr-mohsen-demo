Inguinal hernia is one of the most common paediatric surgical conditions: a small opening in the abdominal wall lets the intestine slide down the inguinal canal — towards a boy's scrotum, or a girl's labial area.

Unlike an umbilical hernia — which can close on its own — an inguinal hernia does not close by itself. That does not make it an emergency when it is caught early: the point of catching it is exactly to avoid the emergency.

### What you actually see at home

- A small soft bulge in the groin or above the scrotum, either side.
- It **grows when the child stands, strains or pushes**, and is most visible while crying.
- It **disappears completely** during sleep and rest.

The same bulge appears, vanishes and returns over months — which is why some families get used to it and stop watching it. That is the one mistake.

### When observation is enough

When the bulge is soft and goes back in easily, the operation is planned normally in regular appointments — no alarms and no urgency. That calm is exactly what an early diagnosis buys you.

### The signs that mean the emergency room now: a strangulated hernia

- A bulge that becomes **hard and will not go back in**, even when the child is calm.
- Continuous crying that nothing settles.
- The bulge turning dark red or purple.
- Vomiting — especially green vomit.
- A sudden refusal to eat.

If more than one sign shows up together, **go to the emergency room immediately**. The gut survives without blood flow for a short window only, and every hour counts.

**The main idea:** an inguinal hernia is not an alarm emergency, but it is a scheduled one — early diagnosis is the difference between a calm morning operation and an emergency in the middle of the night.

### How is it confirmed?

The clinical examination by a paediatric surgeon is the foundation, and when there is any doubt the doctor orders an ultrasound, which shows the contents of the canal. The diagnosis is not difficult, and the operation is prepared as a routine procedure the doctor explains to the whole family before the day.
